Simulate Before You Integrate
How a virtual clinic reveals what happens when AI meets a real breast imaging workflow — before a single patient is affected.
The problem
Mammogram looks uncertain?
She waits days. Then comes back.
A BI-RADS 0 result sends patients home for weeks of anxiety — and some never return at all. AI can read scans in real time. So why not keep her there for same-day follow-up?
The catch
A clinic is a finely-tuned machine

Same-day diagnostic imaging
Add one new step and radiologists, equipment, and schedules all compete differently. The ripple effects are invisible until it's too late.
The solution
Build a virtual clinic and run it hundreds of times
Discrete event simulation — grounded in real data from UCLA's Barbara Kort Women's Imaging Center — lets us test "what if" scenarios in minutes, not months.

The fix · AMIA 2024 Study 2
The right configuration almost eliminates disruption
Morning-only AI module (9am–12pm) All 4 radiologists stay flexible Only −4% volume or +2% hours
Simulation also modeled financial costs of reserving same-day slots — turning outputs into numbers administrators can actually weigh.
Study 3 · JACR 2025
Schedule higher-risk patients first
Using AI risk scores + patient data, scheduling templates sequence patients by cancer risk — giving those most likely to need same-day imaging the best chance of getting it. More efficient. More equitable.
Open source
MammoDES — free to adapt for your clinic
All simulation frameworks are on GitHub, written in Python with SimPy. Toggle the AI module, test radiologist assignments, experiment with morning vs afternoon workflows.
The takeaway
Diagnostic accuracy alone isn't enough.
System-level design matters.
Simulation lets us pressure-test AI integration while the stakes are still hypothetical. Not to predict the future — but to ask the right questions before committing to irreversible change.
